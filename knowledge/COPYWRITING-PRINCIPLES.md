# COPYWRITING-PRINCIPLES.md

Reference file for Charlie. Synthesised from Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*. Applies to short-form DTC copy: email (<200 words), social ads (<125 chars), landing pages (<300 words), Google Ads (<30 chars).

**Before writing a single word, identify two things from the brief:**
1. **Awareness Level** (Section 1) — determines the headline's job
2. **Sophistication Stage** (Section 2) — determines what angle is NOT already exhausted

Grep this file for those two terms. Don't read top-to-bottom.

---

## 1. Market Awareness Levels

Schwartz's most critical framework. Where the audience starts determines where the headline can start. Starting too far ahead of them = the message fails.

| Level | Definition | Headline's job | Body's job | How to spot it in the brief |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Most aware** | Already knows the product, already wants it | **Name product + offer.** Reminder, discount, urgency | Reduce friction to purchase. Shipping, bundle, CTA | "Returning customers", "branded search", "cart abandoners", specific-product searches |
| **Product aware** | Knows the product/category, not yet convinced | **Sharpen one proof point** that closes the gap between consider and buy | Differentiation, reviews, comparison, one killer benefit | "Category shopper", "comparison searches", "retargeting pool" |
| **Solution aware** | Knows the outcome they want, doesn't know your product | **Name the outcome + hint at a new route** | Introduce product as the specific route. Mechanism matters here | "Wants to fix X", "searching for how to X", "has tried other things" |
| **Problem aware** | Feels the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | **Name the pain specifically.** Agitate, don't explain yet | Reveal that a solution exists. Problem → mechanism → product | "Frustrated with X", "struggling to X", pain-point targeting |
| **Completely unaware** | Not thinking about the problem at all | **Curiosity, identity, or story hook.** Do NOT mention product | Interrupt pattern, earn attention, reframe reader's world | Broad cold traffic, lookalike audiences, interest targeting |

### Example headlines by level (modern, hydration powder)

> **Most aware:** "20% off your tub today + free shipping"
>
> **Product aware:** "The electrolyte ratio trainers actually recommend (not the sugar-water 'sports drinks')"
>
> **Solution aware:** "Wake up less flat, foggy, and behind — even after poor sleep"
>
> **Problem aware:** "Why you still feel wrecked after 8 glasses of water"
>
> **Completely unaware:** "Your brain is 73% water. Guess what happens when it's dehydrated."

**Charlie's rule: match the headline to where the reader actually is. A problem-aware headline to a most-aware audience wastes their time. A most-aware headline to an unaware audience gets scrolled past.**

## 2. Market Sophistication Stages

Schwartz's second-most-critical framework. How many times has this market heard this claim? That answers what still works and what is now exhausted.

| Stage | Market state | What works | What stops working | Modern example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | First mover, no competitors | **Direct claim.** "Product does X." | Nothing — every direction is open | A genuinely new category (first-to-market) |
| **Stage 2** | Copycats arrive | **Enlarge the claim.** Bigger, faster, stronger, more | The simple claim alone | "10× stronger than standard" |
| **Stage 3** | Market saturated with claims | **Introduce a new mechanism.** Why your product works differently | Bigger claims. Audience has become numb | "Our triple-mineral formula works because…" |
| **Stage 4** | Mechanisms have also been copied | **Enlarge or refine the mechanism.** Sharper specificity | Generic mechanisms. Must get more precise | "The only formula with the 4:1 magnesium-to-glycinate ratio" |
| **Stage 5** | Market exhausted. Belief in any mechanism is weak | **Identity / attitude / tribe.** Who this is FOR, not what it does | All mechanism copy. All enlarged claims | "Night support for people who want to feel clear the next morning — not knocked out" |

### Charlie's differentiation check

Before writing:
- **What stage is this category at?** (Skincare, supplements, hydration, sleep = Stage 4-5 in most geos)
- **What angle are competitors using right now?** Ads in feed, PDP hero, email subject lines
- **Am I about to write a Stage 1 headline in a Stage 4 market?** If yes, stop and re-angle

**Charlie's rule: do not repeat what the market has already heard. If every competitor leads with "better sleep," you must lead with either a specific new mechanism or a specific identity.**

## 3. Mass Desire (what to channel, not create)

**Advertising does not create desire. It channels desire that already exists.**

The copywriter's job is to identify the strongest existing desire in the audience, then present the product as the most believable route to fulfilling it.

### How to identify the desire in the brief

In the Audience Context, look for:
- **Permanent drivers** — health, beauty, status, relief, comfort, safety, belonging, mastery
- **Current frustrations** — what's draining, costing, embarrassing, limiting them right now
- **Aspirational states** — how they want to feel, be seen, or function
- **Forces of change** — cultural shifts, trends, new language, new social permissions

**If the brief doesn't make the dominant desire obvious, ask for it. Do not invent one.**

### 5 desire intensification techniques

| Technique | What it does | One-line example |
|---|---|---|
| **Name the pain specifically** | Makes the current state feel recognisable and costly | "For nights when you're exhausted but your brain won't switch off" |
| **Vivid the outcome** | Makes the future state emotionally concrete | "Wake up calm and clear, not foggy and already behind" |
| **Sharpen the before/after contrast** | Expands the felt gap, making action feel worth it | "Stop dragging through the first 3 hours of your day" |
| **Add specificity** | Numbers, timing, scenarios make it real and believable | "Feel it in 4 nights, not 4 weeks" |
| **Name the stakes** | What's lost if nothing changes — without melodrama | "Another year of relying on caffeine to function" |

### Application by channel

- **Email subject lines (<45 chars):** Name one pain or one outcome. Not both. "Why 8 glasses doesn't fix it"
- **Social ad headlines (<125 chars):** Pain + implied solution. "Exhausted but wired at night? You're not broken — you're depleted."
- **Landing page hero (<10 words):** Vivid desired state. "Wake up less wrecked."
- **Google Ads (<30 chars):** Direct desire match. "Sleep without the hangover"

**Charlie's rule: channel the desire already in the brief. Every claim must intensify it, not just state a feature.**

## 4. Headline Frameworks (top 10)

Schwartz has 38. Charlie needs 10. Each is a pattern, a use-case, and one example.

| # | Pattern | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Specific outcome with timing** | Solution-aware + Stage 3-4 | "Fall asleep in 15 minutes without feeling drugged" |
| 2 | **Named contrarian mechanism** | Stage 3-4 crowded category | "The protein ratio that trainers won't shut up about" |
| 3 | **"Why [pain] still happens even when [common fix]"** | Problem-aware, market is tired of generic fixes | "Why you're still tired after 8 hours of sleep" |
| 4 | **Identity-first statement** | Stage 5, exhausted market | "For people who work out tired and want to stop." |
| 5 | **Specific before/after transformation** | Solution-aware, mid-funnel | "From foggy-till-noon to locked-in by 9am" |
| 6 | **Measured proof claim** | Product-aware, needs one convincing hit | "Independent lab test: 4.2× more bioavailable" |
| 7 | **Permission reframe** | Problem-aware, shame or effort barrier | "You don't have to grind through the afternoon crash" |
| 8 | **Curiosity gap with stakes** | Unaware / top of funnel | "The one macro most people under-eat by 40%" |
| 9 | **Direct deal** | Most-aware only | "20% off + free shipping — today" |
| 10 | **Question that sharpens a pattern** | Problem or solution aware | "Ever finished a workout and felt worse than before?" |

### Channel mapping

- **Email subject lines (<45 chars):** #3, #4, #8, #10 work best. Curiosity and identity open. Skip direct-claim in mature markets.
- **Social ad primary text (<125 chars):** #1, #2, #5, #7 — pattern-interrupts that still deliver the promise
- **Landing page headlines (<10 words):** #1, #4, #5, #6 — commit to the angle, don't hedge
- **Google Ads (<30 chars):** #6, #9 — intent traffic wants proof or offer

**Charlie's rule: pick the pattern that matches awareness level × sophistication stage. Don't freestyle — angle first, framework second.**

## 5. Copy Mechanics (sentence-level)

Short-form has no room to waste. 5 techniques, one example each.

### 5.1 Open with what the reader is already thinking

Match the opening line to the internal monologue of the reader at their awareness level.

> ❌ "Meet [product]. It's packed with nutrients."
>
> ✅ "You've done the 'drink more water' thing. Still flat by 11am."

### 5.2 Build proof without length

In 150 words, you can't include a case study. Use one of these proof compressions:

- **Single specific number:** "4× absorption in lab tests"
- **One named expert or source:** "The ratio [known doctor/trainer] uses"
- **Time-stamped outcome:** "Most feel it in 4 nights"
- **Social proof fragment:** "23,000 five-star reviews" (if true)

**Never pad with adjectives. One concrete proof beats five abstract claims.**

### 5.3 CTA psychology by awareness level

| Awareness | CTA type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Most aware | **Direct** | "Order now" / "Reorder" |
| Product aware | **Reassuring** | "Try it risk-free" / "See reviews" |
| Solution aware | **Diagnostic** | "See if it fits your sleep pattern" |
| Problem aware | **Curiosity + low commit** | "See why this works" / "Take the quiz" |
| Unaware | **Content, not product** | "Read the 3-minute guide" |

**Charlie's rule: unaware audiences don't click "Buy Now." Aware audiences don't need to "Read More."**

### 5.4 Rhythm for mobile scan-reading

- **Sentence 1:** Short. Hook.
- **Sentence 2:** Can be longer. Delivers the claim.
- **Sentence 3:** Short again. Punch.
- Paragraphs max 2–3 lines. Blank line between. Bold one phrase per paragraph if needed for scanners.

### 5.5 Specificity beats cleverness

> ❌ "Elevate your mornings." (clever, empty)
>
> ✅ "First 90 minutes, fog-free." (specific, vivid, believable)

**A specific truth beats a clever line every time. Clever is decoration. Specific is conviction.**

## 6. Application Rules for Charlie

Before submitting any copy for review, verify:

```
- [ ] I read the brief's Audience + Audience Context BEFORE writing a word
- [ ] I identified the awareness level and matched my HEADLINE to it
- [ ] I identified the sophistication stage and did NOT write an exhausted angle
- [ ] My headline channels existing desire — I did not try to create a new one
- [ ] My opening line would make the reader continue (not just inform)
- [ ] Every claim intensifies desire or adds proof — no filler adjectives
- [ ] My CTA matches the awareness level (see section 5.3)
- [ ] Word count is within channel limits (email <200, ad primary <125, LP <300, Google <30)
- [ ] One concrete proof point, not five abstract claims
- [ ] Rhythm works on mobile scan-reading (short / longer / short)
```

### When in doubt

1. **If the brief doesn't name the dominant desire → ask.** Don't invent one.
2. **If you can't tell the sophistication stage → scan 3 competitor ads.** Write counter to what you see.
3. **If the copy feels flat → intensify the pain or the outcome** (section 3). Don't add adjectives.
4. **If the CTA feels wrong → check awareness level** (section 5.3). It's probably mismatched.
5. **If every variation sounds the same → you're stuck in the wrong sophistication stage.** Move to a fresher angle (Stage 3 → Stage 5).
