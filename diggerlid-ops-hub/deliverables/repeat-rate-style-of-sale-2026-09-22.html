1 · The answer in one page

Low repeat, on the Hybrid line

Classification: DiggerLid is a Low-repeat store that sits on the boundary of Hybrid. 17.7% of all customers have ever bought twice; on the last-12-month returning-customer measure it is 21.4%. Grease is the only thing that gets us to the line. Remove it and we are Low with no ambiguity. PRO Mat on its own is deep Low.

Why it matters

In EE terms a Low-repeat store wins on first-order economics: average order value, first-order gross profit after marketing, and immediate payback of acquisition cost. That matches our cohort finding that 12-month lifetime value is roughly the first order. Grease is the single retention lever with any mass. Our sales, however, behave like a Hybrid store's sales (section 5), which changes how we should forecast them but not which levers we pull.

Grease adds four to five points of repeat rate and is the only reason the blended store reaches the Hybrid line. Within the last 12 months alone, non-grease buyers produced just 6.7% additional orders; most of the 16.4% is older customers coming back for a second machine or an accessory, not a reorder cycle.

Repeat by product set (all time, extra orders per buyer)

Distinct orders containing the set ÷ distinct buyers of the set, minus one. This is the closest live measure of repeat at product level and an upper bound on the share of buyers with two or more orders of that set.

Outliers worth knowing

  • Reorderers switch variant. Grease Packs repeat at 26.8% as a product but no single variant exceeds 15% (LZR2 13.7%, LC002 10.3%, HD800 8.1%). Second orders change size or grade. The 40-piece packs are the stickiest variants (14 to 15%). Hammer Paste: 0% repeat across 93 buyers.
  • The battery adapter's 8.4% is a second-platform effect. Milwaukee 7.1%, Makita 3.1%, DeWALT 0.9%: people buy a second adapter for another battery system, not a replacement.
  • Cheap and universal covers repeat like fleet buyers. Universal covers 9.3% and Quicky 7.2% at product level versus 2% for the premium 1.7-tonne cover: extra machines, not replacements.

What PRO Mat is

It is the purest acquisition product in the range: more than nine in ten buyers meet the brand through it. Once bought it is one-and-done, with roughly 2% buying another mat and 5% buying anything else within its first 13 months. There is no retention layer to invest in. The only repeat path is a post-purchase cross-sell into the grease system, which is where the store's repeat behaviour actually lives.

Run PRO Mat entirely on first-order economics: average order value through the PLUS and colour mix and bundles, gross profit after marketing at the first order, acquisition cost paid back on day one.

How to use the classification

You would tick Hybrid on the form (12-month returning-customer rate 21.4%) but you should run the business as Low repeat. The margin is a few points and it is entirely grease.

Low-repeat levers: first-order AOV (bundles, PLUS mix, percentage-plus-gift offers rather than flat dollars), first-order gross profit after marketing, acquisition efficiency measured on first-order payback, conversion rate and revenue per visitor.

The one Hybrid lever: grease reorder. Roughly one in four grease-pack buyers reorders, and bulk (40-piece) buyers are the stickiest. A restock reminder at around 90 days, a bulk-size nudge, or a subscription is the only retention programme with enough customers behind it to move the store-level number. Crossing 25% on the 12-month measure would make the Hybrid label genuine.

Do not build retention programmes for durables. Covers, enclosures, DiggerShield and PRO Mat repeat at 2 to 11% and the repeat that exists is multi-machine owners, not loyalty.

Method note: repeat rates use Shopify's new-versus-returning order flag and the customers dataset (orders per customer), both exact. Product-set figures use distinct orders ÷ distinct buyers and are upper bounds. Free gifts and package protection are excluded from product tables. About 3,000 buyers sit under legacy product titles and are unattributed at product level.

6 · Our Hybrid sale pattern

Hybrid-shaped sales on a Low-repeat base

Our two big sales do not look like the Low-repeat curve. The mid-sale plateau sits at about 5% of sale revenue per day in both, which is the Hybrid signature (Low-repeat stores run a flat 8 to 9%). Each sale concentrates about 30% of its revenue into one 48-hour spike; BFCM puts it at the launch, EOFY puts it at the June 30 tax deadline.

6 · Our Hybrid sale pattern (continued)

Who buys at the spikes

In the EE model a Hybrid or High-repeat sale spikes because the loyal base buys the moment the sale opens. That is not what happens here. The returning-customer share of revenue during both sales is the same as an ordinary month, and the peak days are 70 to 74% new customers. The spike is urgency acting on acquisition traffic (paid social plus email reach), not the base coming back.

Returning-customer share of net revenue by sale phase, against the August 2026 business-as-usual month.

What it means

  • Forecast with the Hybrid curve. About 30% of sale revenue in the first 48 hours, roughly 5% per day through the middle, 10 to 15% in the last 48 hours when Cyber Monday closes it. On a $605k November that is about $180k for the launch weekend and $30k a day on the plateau.
  • Pull Low-repeat levers. Launch-day buyers are 70% new, so launch creative and paid budget are acquisition plays. Do not starve prospecting on day one to protect retargeting.
  • The plateau is the upside. The Knock-Off 3:30 daily-drop mechanic is a plateau-lifter; measure it against the 5% per day baseline.

Why EOFY and BFCM are mirror images

The urgency sits at opposite ends. BFCM's urgency is the launch itself. EOFY's is an external deadline, so customers wait: the last day alone did $109k on 356 orders, 74% of them new. Same total, same plateau, opposite skew. Plan BFCM to front-load spend and stock; plan EOFY to back-load them.

Email moves who buys

Segmented sends to the engaged base lifted returning share to 43% on Cyber Monday and 42% on EOFY day nine. Full-database blasts did not move it. One engaged-segment send mid-sale and one at close are worth more than another blast to everyone.

Caveats: two sales only, 14-day windows against EE's 12-day reference (positions are normalised in the chart). Launch-day share depends on launch hour; both launched early afternoon. Black Friday itself shows 18 returning orders netting to $411 (refunds), treated as noise.
