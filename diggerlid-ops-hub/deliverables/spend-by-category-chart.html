The read
Spend chased volume, not efficiency: Grease ran the best ROAS all year (5.4 → 2.4) on a shrinking budget share, while Portable got the July cash injection at its worst-ever ROAS (1.44). The budget is mapped upside-down to both efficiency and retention.

Cart → purchase rate

Purchases ÷ add-to-carts, by category. Read with care: Meta's add-to-cart pixel under-fired through H1, so ratios above the 100% funnel ceiling are a tracking gap, not reality. Trust the H2 trend.

The read (H2, reliable window)
Once tracking stabilised, cart→purchase converged to ~26–29% across all three categories (Jul: Portable 27% · Grease 26% · Covers 29%). So cart-close is not where the categories differ — the efficiency gap is upstream, in click quality, CPA and AOV, not in how well each product closes from cart.

Outbound clicks

Clicks that leave Meta for the site, by category — a proxy for demand/interest the ads generate.

The read
Covers pull the most click interest all year (and a big July spike to ~51k) — yet convert at only mid ROAS (1.90). That's a merchandising/AOV opportunity, not a demand problem. Portable's July click surge (~27k) landed right as its ROAS collapsed — we paid for clicks that didn't convert.

Accounting for July's traffic surge

July's efficiency collapse wasn't the site breaking — it was a deliberate volume push into Covers + Portable prospecting that bought a wave of lower-intent clicks. Traffic jumped; return per click fell.

What actually happened
Paid outbound clicks rose ~50% MoM to ~100k, led by Covers (+118%) and Portable (+89%); Grease clicks fell 23%. Yet ROAS fell in both scaled lines (Covers 2.83→1.90, Portable 2.59→1.44) while cart→purchase held ~27–29% — the checkout closed fine. So the extra traffic was cheaper but lower-intent and lower-AOV, poured mostly into Portable (the one-and-done, AOV-diluting line). Same story on the Shopify side: 116,420 sessions (+52% vs May) at just ~$3.15 revenue/session and 1.42% CVR. July = far more traffic at worse quality — that's the MER blowout, not a conversion or site fault.

Smoking gun + the refined fix
The LPV "Flagship" ad set bought 16,335 clicks — 16% of all July traffic — for just $1,300, because it's optimized for landing-page views, not purchases (1 purchase, ROAS 0.07). Near-free vanity traffic that converts ~zero. Combined with broad Covers/Grease/PRO-Mat prospecting (ROAS 0.84–0.91), the broad top-funnel layer is the whole traffic-surge-without-conversion problem. The fix sharpens: cut broad prospecting + LPV across products, protect the warm TOM layer — which converts at ROAS 2.9–4.3, including PRO Mat's own warm ad set (2.92). It's a funnel-stage problem as much as a product one.

Decomposition of the surge
Instagram + under-35 audiences drove the low-converting July click volume; the profitable traffic is Facebook + 35–54 + male. Combined with the funnel read above (broad prospecting + LPV), the recovery is clear: shift budget toward Facebook, 35–54, warm/TOM audiences, and cut broad IG/young/LPV prospecting.
