## #1 TAKE COVER — Paintball / Under Attack  🎨
**Big idea:** Black Friday as a full paintball ambush. Deals fly like paint — get behind something or wear it. The only safe machine on site is a covered one.
**Through-line:** paint splatter = every hazard gear cops (mud, dust, sun, swarf) made visible and fun. **The demo IS the product proof:** splatter an uncovered digger next to a covered one; pull the cover — spotless.
**Lines:** `TAKE COVER — YOU'RE UNDER ATTACK` · `INCOMING: DEALS FROM EVERY DIRECTION` · `GET HIT OR GET COVERED` · `DIRECT HITS ON PRICES. ZERO HITS ON YOUR GEAR.`
**Visual system:** paint splats in **DiggerLid yellow only, on black** — one accent used violently. Goggle/mask POV frames. Ambush cadence for email/SMS: Scouts spotted → Under fire → Direct hit (sale live) → Ceasefire (last hours).
**Content:** July paintball shoots (calendar: Paintball Pt 1 & 2) slot straight in — hero film + stills + BTS.
**Watch:** playful-tradie not tactical/military; splatter stays yellow-on-black or the palette dissolves.

## #2 ALL AUSSIE ADVENTURES — outback mockumentary  🇦🇺
**Big idea:** loving parody of the iconic outback-bumbler format: our own khaki-clad "expert" tours Australia dispensing machinery wisdom and wrecking everything *except* the covered gear. The covers are the only competent thing on screen.
**Through-line:** every segment ends in disaster around the machine while the cover shrugs it off. "Whatever he does to it — it's covered."
**Lines:** `ALL AUSSIE ADVENTURES IN MACHINERY PROTECTION` · "Now, a lot of blokes will tell ya a tarp'll do. Those blokes are wrong." · `BIG COUNTRY. BIGGER SAVINGS.` · episode cards ("Ep 3: The Pilbara Incident").
**IP handling (keeps the vibe, kills the risk):** build **our own recurring character** (e.g. "Gravel Grant") — khaki, hat, moustache energy, reverse-over-the-esky physical comedy. Parody the *format/archetype*; never use the show's name, the actor's name/likeness, specific scenes, or logo in paid media.
**Best for:** long-form social video, virality, Charlie-heartland brand love. Weakest pure urgency — pair with hard offer end-cards.

## #3 BRICK BY BRICK — "Big Kids. Bigger Toys."  🧱
**Big idea:** diggers are grown men's toy diggers at 1:1 scale — and every kid knows the rule: look after your toys and **pack them away when you're done**. Toy care = machine care = covers. Lands doubly at Christmas.
**Lines:** `BIG KIDS. BIGGER TOYS.` · `PACK YOUR TOYS AWAY PROPERLY` · `SOME ASSEMBLY REQUIRED. PROTECTION INCLUDED.` · `THE TOYS GREW UP. SO DID THE TOY BOX.`
**Visual system:** brick-built excavator builds on black; stud/pixel motifs; brick yellow ≈ brand yellow — practically brand-native.
**Best for:** thumb-stopping social; the gift-buyer segment; bundles framed as "sets".
**IP line (strict):** never the LEGO name, logo, or minifigures. Generic "brick-built" aesthetic only. Production heavier (custom builds/renders).

## #4 COVERS ON! — Aussie summer cricket  🏏
**Big idea:** clouds roll in at the G and the cry goes up — **"Covers on!"** The ground staff sprint; the pitch gets protected. Same rule on your site. The only sporting moment on earth whose hero is literally a protective cover.
**Timing:** BFCM lands as the Test summer starts — **Nov 2026 is an Ashes summer in Australia.**
**Lines:** `COVERS ON!` · `RAIN'S COMING. DEALS ARE HERE.` · `THE GROUND STAFF DON'T MUCK AROUND. NEITHER SHOULD YOU.` · `PROTECT THE PITCH` · `BAD LIGHT NEVER STOPPED A SALE`.
**Visual system:** ground-staff sprint energy with machines instead of pitches; black scoreboard graphics with yellow lettering; sale phases as session times (First Session = early access · Tea = mid-sale bonus · Final Session = last hours).
**Fit:** dead-centre Charlie + cricket-dad Christmas gift buyer. Generic cricket = no IP issue (no team/broadcaster marks). AU-only resonance (fine).

## #5 KNOCK-OFF DEALS  🍺
**Big idea:** the sacred double meaning. **Knock-off** = best moment of a tradie's day (tools down, 3:30). **Knockoff** = the cheap imitation gear you'd never put on your machine. One sale owns both: *genuine gear at prices that look like knockoffs — dropping at knock-off time.*
**Through-line:** DiggerLid's pitch is anti-knockoff (real covers vs the $40 tarp). BF is the one week the *price* looks fake but the *gear* isn't. "The only knockoff on this site is the time."
**Mechanic (the engine):** **a new deal drops at knock-off, 3:30 PM sharp, every day of the sale.** Daily return traffic; SMS moment ("TOOLS DOWN. DEALS UP."); a countdown that means something culturally.
**Lines:** `KNOCK-OFF DEALS. GENUINE GEAR.` · `TOOLS DOWN. PRICES DOWN.` · `NEW DEAL EVERY DAY AT KNOCK-OFF (3:30 SHARP)` · `PRICES SO LOW THEY LOOK LIKE KNOCKOFFS. THEY'RE NOT.` · `DON'T BUY A KNOCKOFF. BUY AT KNOCK-OFF.` · Dec-tail closer: `LAST KNOCK-OFF OF THE YEAR — PARK IT PROTECTED.`
**Visual system:** 3:30 wall clock in yellow-on-black; esky lids, utes at the gate, tools-down silhouettes; recurring **"KNOCK-OFF: 3:30"** stamp badge (Impact, yellow banner) as the daily-drop mark; gag shot — shredded no-name tarp labelled `KNOCKOFF` beside a DiggerLid cover labelled `KNOCK-OFF PRICE`.
**Fit:** fluent tradie; the anti-counterfeit angle lets quality-first buyers take the discount without feeling like cheap-gear buyers — the needle BF usually can't thread.
**Risks:** 3:30 drops need 7 days of operational discipline (scheduled Klaviyo sends + site updates); "knockoff" must always resolve to *genuine* in the same breath.
