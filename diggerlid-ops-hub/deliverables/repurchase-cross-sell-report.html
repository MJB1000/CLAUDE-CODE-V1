Repurchase & Cross-Sell: who comes back, and for what

Cohort retention study · Prepared 2 Aug 2026 · Source: Shopify Admin API (live), full order histories · Deterministic title-based classification · Wilson 95% CIs, two-proportion z-tests

We pulled every order for two groups of new customers and followed them for the better part of a year. The question: after someone's first purchase, do they come back — and do they ever move up to the big-ticket gear? The short answer: grease keeps them coming back, covers sell once with a small tail, and mats are one-and-done.

How to read this

Three plain-English measures, applied identically to every group:

  • Repurchase — did they ever place a second order (of anything)?
  • Cross-sell — did a later order include a different category than they started in?
  • High-value repurchase — did a later order include any item over $100?

Cohort A: first order Aug–Oct 2025 (n=893, 9–12 months observed). Cohort B: the BFCM PRO Mat launch, first order Nov 30 2025–Feb 2026 (mats n=77, ~8 months). Categories assigned by product title; a customer's "first purchase" is the highest-value line in their first order.

Read it this way
Grease is the retention engine (high frequency, mostly of more grease). Covers are a single sale with a small high-value tail (~10–12%). Mats are a pure single transaction — no LTV tail at all.

Is grease a gateway to covers? No.

The theory: pay more to acquire a grease customer because they'll graduate to a cover. We tested it at scale — n=462 grease-first, ±2pt precision.

  • Grease-first buyers reach a >$100 non-grease order at just 5.2% [3.5–7.6] — and that's no higher than a cover buyer's own repeat rate (7.9%, p=0.10).
  • Sub-types are indistinguishable: coupler-only 6.4% (n=78) vs bundles 5.6% (n=340), p=0.78. The earlier "coupler-only never converts" was small-sample noise (n=4).

Verdict: don't pay a premium CPA betting on grease → cover migration. It mostly doesn't happen.

What it means for spend

1
Mat CPA must pay back on order one. At ~2.6% repurchase there's no second order to recover acquisition cost. Funding mat prospecting is a list-building decision, not an LTV one — size it that way.
2
Treat covers as single-sale-plus-a-small-tail. ~12–14% come back, ~10–12% for another big-ticket item. Bid cover CPA to a single-sale payback and treat the tail as upside, not the plan.
3
Bid grease on frequency, never migration. Grease's edge is high-repeat consumable reorders. The grease→cover jump (5.2% ±2) doesn't justify a premium CPA.
4
This is the flip side of the AOV story. PRO Mat drove volume but replaced repeat-capable enclosure buyers with one-and-done mat buyers at a third of the price — hurting on both first-order value and repeat.

Honest caveats

  • Mats n=77 (±7pt) vs covers n=374 — wider interval, but the gap is large enough that repurchase and high-value repurchase both clear significance.
  • The mat sample is the BFCM launch cohort — chosen because it's the longest-observed and highest-volume mat group. Newer mat buyers have had less time to repeat, so pulling them would bias the number down, not tighten it fairly. The clean fix is to re-observe this same cohort in a few months.
  • Cross-sell is measured at the category level; a cheaper same-category repeat won't count as cross-sell (by design).

Method: Shopify Admin GraphQL, live 2 Aug 2026.
