How to load a grease gun depends on the cartridge. With an old ring-pull tube you pull the plunger back, swap the tube, screw the head on and bleed out the air. With a screw-top cartridge you unscrew the empty, screw on the full one and pump. The screw-top way takes under 20 seconds and keeps grease off your hands.

We're Luke and Joel, two landscaping tradies who started DiggerLid in 2018, and we grease our own machines every week. For our Grease VS video we put both systems side by side in the shed: one brother greasing like it's 1966, the other like it's 2026. Here is everything that shoot taught us.

The short version

  • Ring-pull tubes in a spring-plunger gun take over a minute to change, plus bleeding and repriming.
  • Screw-top cartridges with an inbuilt follower change over in less than 20 seconds and use 100% of the grease.
  • Most priming problems come from air getting in during the swap. Fewer open ends means fewer chances for air.
  • Check your cartridge size before you buy. A 400g tube won't seat properly in a 450g barrel.

How to load a grease gun with a ring-pull cartridge

To load a standard spring-plunger grease gun, pull the plunger rod back and lock it, unscrew the barrel, drop in the new tube with its cap off, rip the ring-pull, screw the barrel back on and release the plunger. Then pump, and bleed out any trapped air until grease flows. It works, it's just slow.

This is the method most of us learned on the tools. The 400g open-ended tube has been in workshops for over 60 years, according to HTS Spares, and the spring-plunger gun design is older than most of the machines it services. In our video, Luke's gun runs on a plunger system he dates to 1966 [CHECK].

  1. Pull the plunger rod all the way back and lock it in the slot so the spring is held.
  2. Unscrew the barrel from the gun head and pull out the empty tube. Expect grease on your fingers here.
  3. Take the plastic cap off the new tube and slide it in, open end first.
  4. Pull the ring-pull seal off the head end. Opening grease should feel like opening a tin of dog food, as Luke puts it.
  5. Screw the barrel back onto the head, but leave it a turn or two loose.
  6. Release the plunger rod so the spring pushes on the tube.
  7. Pump the handle while you tighten the barrel. If your gun has a bleeder valve, crack it open until grease shows, then close it.
  8. Pump onto a rag until you get a steady flow, not spits of air.

Because the spring keeps constant pressure on an open tube, grease tends to squeeze out around the seals. Luke's line from the shoot sums it up: "I change the tube, bleed it, prime it, have a little swear."

How do you load a screw-top grease cartridge?

A screw-top grease cartridge loads in three moves: unscrew the empty cartridge from the gun head, take the cap off the new one, and screw it on. The cartridge has its own follower inside and the gun draws grease by suction, so there's no spring or barrel to wrestle. KAJO rates the changeover at less than 20 seconds.

  1. Unscrew the empty cartridge from the gun head.
  2. Unscrew the cap from the new 500g cartridge.
  3. Screw the new cartridge straight into the head until it's snug. Don't over-tighten.
  4. Pump a few strokes onto a rag to confirm a steady flow, then get back on the machine.

On our shoot, Joel had the new tube on and pumping while Luke was still hunting for the ring-pull. His line: "Unscrew one, screw on the next, back to work." The follower inside each cartridge pushes right to the end, so you use 100% of the grease instead of binning a tube with a smear left in it.

Ring-pull vs screw-top: which is faster and cleaner?

Screw-top cartridges win on speed, mess and waste. A ring-pull tube in a spring-plunger gun takes over a minute to swap, then needs bleeding. A screw-top cartridge changes in less than 20 seconds and stays sealed until it's in the gun. Ring-pull tubes are cheaper per kilo and fit almost any standard gun, which is why they hang around.

Why does a grease gun lose prime, and how do you fix it?

A grease gun loses prime when air gets between the grease and the pump. That usually happens during a cartridge swap, from an air pocket in the tube, or when the gun is stored nozzle-up and grease sags away from the outlet. The fix is to push the air out: loosen the head, pump until grease shows, then tighten.

  • Air in after a swap. Loosen the barrel or cartridge a turn, pump until grease weeps from the thread, then nip it up.
  • Air pockets in the tube. Push the follower or plunger rod up by hand to force grease to the outlet before you pump.
  • Running the tube dry. Change cartridges before they're completely empty, or you'll fill the hose with air.
  • Storage. Store the gun lying flat or nozzle-down, not hanging with the head up.
  • Stiff grease in the cold. Warm the cartridge in the cab for a few minutes on a frosty morning.

Screw-top systems cut down on priming because the cartridge stays sealed. They aren't magic, though. One of our own reviewers has had a KAJO gun lose prime. The fix is the same idea: push up on the follower and pump until grease shows, so the air has gone. Joel's claim on camera is that his gun is designed to stay primed, and that holds once the air is out.

What size grease cartridge fits my gun?

In Australia, standard grease guns take 400g or 450g cartridges, and they are not interchangeable. Screw-top systems use their own threads and sizes, like KAJO's 500g tube. Check the barrel or the manual before you buy, because a cartridge that nearly fits will leak, air-lock or refuse to seat.

Common grease gun loading mistakes

The most common grease gun loading mistakes are buying the wrong size tube, priming with the barrel done up tight, running cartridges dry and fighting the coupler with one hand. Most greasing headaches are self-inflicted. We made every one of these before we switched systems.

  1. Buying the wrong size tube. Check 400g vs 450g before you leave the shop, or you're driving back.
  2. Leaving the barrel tight while priming. Air needs a way out. Back it off a turn first.
  3. Pumping the tube bone dry. You'll fill the hose with air and prime all over again.
  4. Holding the coupler on with one hand. A quick release grease coupler locks onto the nipple, punches through blocked ones and keeps one hand free. It's rated to 10,000 PSI and comes with 1/8 NPT and BSP thread adapters.
  5. Mixing parts from three shops. A gun from one store, a coupler off a marketplace and grease from another rarely play nicely. As Luke says: "Every part works. Just not necessarily together."
  6. Swapping grease grades mid-tube in an open-ended gun. With a sealed screw-top cartridge you can unscrew a part-used tube and fit a different grade, then purge the line [CHECK].
  7. Wiping your hands on your shirt. Keep Digger Wipes heavy duty hand wipes in the ute for the mess you can't avoid.

How to load a grease gun FAQs

How do you load a grease gun without getting air in it?

Leave the barrel or cartridge a turn loose, push the plunger or follower up until grease shows at the thread, then tighten while pumping. Change cartridges before they run completely empty. Sealed screw-top cartridges help most, because there is no open tube end for air to sneak in during the swap.

Why won't my grease gun pump after changing the cartridge?

It has almost always air-locked. Air trapped between the new tube and the pump stops the pump from drawing grease. Loosen the head a turn, press the bleeder valve if your gun has one, and pump until grease flows. If it still won't go, check the tube is the right size and seated fully.

Are 400g and 450g grease cartridges interchangeable?

No. The two sizes have different diameters, so a 400g tube won't seat properly in a 450g barrel and a 450g tube is usually too big for a 400g gun. Check the label on the barrel or the manual. Screw-top cartridges sidestep this, because they screw straight into a matching gun head.

How long does it take to change a grease cartridge?

With a ring-pull tube in a spring-plunger gun, plan on over a minute, plus however long bleeding takes on the day. A KAJO screw-top cartridge changes over in less than 20 seconds. Across a fleet greased weekly, those minutes add up to real hours back on paid work.

Do screw-top grease cartridges waste less grease?

Yes. Each KAJO cartridge has an inbuilt follower that pushes right to the end, so you use 100% of the grease. Open-ended ring-pull tubes leave residue stuck to the walls and ends, which goes in the bin with the empty. Less waste also means less grease smeared over the gun and ute.
