# DiggerLid Tone of Voice: Blog Edition

Load this with `article-blueprint.md` for every blog draft. Every quote below exists verbatim in the live site scrape (7 Oct 2026, 88 pages), the Meta ad references or the review bank. Page paths are given so you can check them. Stats come from `scratchpad/scripts/tov_stats.py` (stdlib, `python3 -I`).

## 1. The voice in one paragraph

DiggerLid writes like a landscaping tradie who has been on the tools and is now showing a mate how it's done. It is plain Australian English: short sentences, real numbers, and one dry joke where it fits. We pick a fight with the problem (soggy cardboard, rain days, the 1966 grease gun) and never with the reader. We back every claim with a spec, a test or a customer's own words. Then we stop talking. On the blog the same bloke has pulled up a milk crate for a proper yarn. He explains more, but he still doesn't waffle.

## 2. Four voice pillars

**Pillar 1. Mate on site (warm, Aussie, a bit cheeky)**
- What it means: talk to one person, in the second person, the way you would at smoko. Use light idiom and a little humour. Save the slang for the right moment.
- Do: "you", contractions, one cheeky line per section, everyday words (ute, smoko, sorted, mate).
- Don't: forced slang, more than one in-joke per section, jokes about the reader's body, age or wallet.
- Real lines: "Warmer than a cuddle from Nan" (/products/diggerlid-workwear-hoodie). "Finally, a DiggerLid Trucker cap to keep the sun off your scone." (/products/diggerlid-trucker-cap)

**Pillar 2. Been on the tools (credibility through specifics)**
- What it means: the founders were landscapers, so write from experience. Use part names, machine models, measurements and what actually happens on site.
- Do: give exact specs (490 GSM, 10mm, 196 x 95cm, 10,000 PSI), name machines (Kubota U17-3, Wacker Neuson EZ17), and say what you tried yourselves.
- Don't: use vague quality words ("superior", "top-notch"), make claims you can't back up, or tell an "epic" origin story.
- Real line: "it was just 2 blokes running a landscape construction business, who hated rocking up to site to find a wet seat on their digger every morning." (/pages/about-us)

**Pillar 3. Dunk on the problem, never the customer**
- What it means: every piece needs a villain, named early. The reader is just doing what he's always done.
- Do: name the enemy (cardboard, rain days, ring-pull tubes, road grime on the hitch). Frame it as old way against new way.
- Don't: call the reader's habits dumb, use "self-inflicted", knock our own older products (the OG mat, the original covers), or open with empathy padding ("We know how frustrating...").
- Real lines: "Still Kneeling On Cardboard?" (/pages/pro-mat-plus-2026). "This isn’t a thin tarp or throw-over cover." (/products/draw-bar-cover)

**Pillar 4. Short, concrete, done**
- What it means: use full stops rather than commas, and stacked short lines. Let the punchline land, then move on.
- Do: use triads and two-beat closers, then end the section.
- Don't: write sentences over 25 words in a row, stack adjectives, write a conclusion that only repeats the article, or use more than one exclamation mark.
- Real lines: "Stay dry. Stay padded. Stay comfortable." (/products/pro-mat). "One less thing to clean. One more thing sorted." (/products/draw-bar-cover)

## 4. The blog dial

The blog uses the same person as the ads, at a different volume. Ads shout across the site. Product pages sell at the counter. The blog teaches in the shed.

| | Ads (Meta) | Product pages | Blog |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Stop the scroll, one click | Answer will-it-fit and is-it-tough, then convert | Answer the search question fully, earn trust, then link |
| Length | 1 to 10 lines | 150 to 600 words | 1,500 to 2,200 words |
| Sentences | Fragments. "Quick. Easy. Back on the job." | Short spec lines plus 1 to 2 sentence benefits | Mostly 8 to 18 words, with a 3 to 6 word punch line each section |
| Person | "You", plus a villain | "You" plus "our" (spec ownership) | First person plural (we tested, on our machines) plus "you", around 2.5:1 |
| Humour | One in-joke per ad | Taglines ("Warmer than a cuddle from Nan") | One cheeky line per H2, at most |
| Emoji | Locked system | Feature icons | None in headings or prose |
| Caps | One CAPS word allowed | Some CAPS headers (tidy up) | Sentence case. No CAPS words |
| Proof | Number plus review snippet | Spec table plus reviews | Method steps, comparison tables, sourced stats, verbatim quotes |
| CTA | "Shop Now" | Add to cart | One soft product block mid-article, one closer. "Fast shipping Australia wide" |

**Blog rule of thumb:** answer first in plain words, then show how, then add one line of personality. Explaining is fine. Waffle is not.

**Product naming rules:**
- Brand: **DiggerLid** (one word, capital L) every time. The site also uses Digger Lid (12x), Diggerlid (4x), DiggerLID, Digger-Lid and DIGGERLID. Never copy those.
- **PRO Mat**, **PRO Mat Plus**, **Pro Enclosure**, **DiggerShield**, **KAJO**:
  - Follow the product title on first mention: PRO Mat, Pro Excavator Enclosure.
  - After that, PRO Mat, Pro Enclosure, DiggerShield (never "Digger Shield"), KAJO (always caps).
  - The landing pages write "Pro Mat Plus". Pick **PRO Mat Plus** on the blog and keep it consistent within an article.
- **Digger Wipes** (two words, the PDP title). Not DiggerWipes.
- **OG** only alongside **Plus**. In prose, say "the original PRO Mat".
- **DigHeads** (customers, plural). The **DIGHEAD Beanie** keeps its product caps.
- **Quicky Cover**, **Draw Bar Cover**, **Micro Excavator Cover**, **1.7 Tonne Excavator Cover**, **Universal / Engine Covers**. Use the live product titles when you link.

**Australian spelling:**
- colour, organise, tonne, metre, tyre, centre, favourite, moulded, aluminium, defence, licence (noun), fertiliser.
- The site slips into US spelling: "revolutionized" and "defense" (/products/digger-lid-1-7), and the "Color" option label (/products/pro-mat, /products/digger-lid-quicky-cover). Never copy these.
- Keep imperial conversions only in spec tables.

**Hard bans:**
- Em dashes, anywhere.
- "elevate", "unlock", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "seamless", "solution", "ultimate", "cutting-edge", "premium-grade", "innovative", "pinnacle", "peace of mind", "rest assured".
- Calling a whole enclosure "sealed".
- "Keeping you safe" or any safety promise. Reflective strips "help you be seen".
- "Made in Australia" for anything except DiggerShield.
- Free-shipping thresholds.
- The phrase "we're diggin' it". It is banned, full stop.

## 7. TOV scoring rubric (self-score every draft)

Score each dimension 0 to 5. **The pass mark is 24/30, with no dimension below 3.**

**Automatic fail, whatever the score:** any em dash, any unresolved `[CHECK]`, any banned word, "we're diggin' it", a free-shipping threshold, or an unsupported claim.

| # | Dimension | 5 looks like | 2 or less looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plain-talk rhythm** | Answer-first. Average 11 to 15 words per sentence, at least 35% under 10, short paragraphs | Long run-on sentences, throat-clearing intros |
| 2 | **On-the-tools credibility** | Real specs, models, method steps, what we tried, honest trade-offs | Generic quality words, no numbers |
| 3 | **Aussie mate warmth** | Natural idiom (1 to 3 per 1,000 words), one cheeky line per H2, you:we 2:1 to 3:1 | Neutral manual voice, or forced slang |
| 4 | **Villain, not victim** | The problem is named early and dunked on. The reader is respected. Old way vs new way | Blames the reader, empathy padding, knocks our own products |
| 5 | **Brand and vocabulary accuracy** | Section 5 naming, AU spelling, preferred terms, no corporate words, no keyword stuffing | Wrong names, US spelling, "solution" talk |
| 6 | **Claims and proof discipline** | Every claim traceable to the catalogue, live PDP or a cited source. Quotes verbatim. Facts and dates verified | Invented stats, unverified biography, missing sources |

**Test article ("How to load a grease gun"): 24/30, scored 5 / 5 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 3. It is BLOCKED by the auto-fail on 2 unresolved `[CHECK]` tags.**
- The rhythm and credibility are excellent: average sentence 12.1 words, 44% under 10, no exclamations, real timings, a comparison table and an honest cost trade-off.
- It loses points on warmth: the only idiom is "ute", "nip it up" and "bone dry".
- It loses points on accuracy:
  - "Most greasing headaches are self-inflicted" blames the reader.
  - "Digger Wipes heavy duty hand wipes" is keyword stuffing.
  - "his brother Joel" is unverified. The USP bank only says "two landscaping tradies".
  - "Updated 15 October 2026" is a future date.
