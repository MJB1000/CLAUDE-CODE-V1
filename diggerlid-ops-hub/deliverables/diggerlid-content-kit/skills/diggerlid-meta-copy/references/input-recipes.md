# Input Recipes

How to turn each kind of source into copy. In every case, finish with the website check (SKILL.md step 3) and the self-check.

## Video transcript or script

1. **Clean it.** Fix transcription errors using the table in SKILL.md. Strip "um", false starts and timecodes.
2. **Mine it.** Pull out:
   - the **hook line**: the first thing said or captioned, which is usually the scroll-stopper
   - every **number** (seconds, dollars, sizes, counts)
   - the **villain** named
   - the **best one-liner**, which becomes a headline candidate because it's already tested on camera
   - the **close or CTA** said on camera
3. **Match the archetype.** A talking head is usually founder POV, customer story or objection. A split-screen is old vs new. Numbers on cards are cost maths. A woman to camera is the gift buyer.
4. **Write the copy to complement the video, not transcribe it.**
   - The primary text should land the hook in words, so it works on mute.
   - Then add what the video can't show: the specs, the warranty, "Fast shipping Australia wide" and the URL.
5. **Keep the creator's lines.** If the talent says something great, quote it. Never put words in a real customer's mouth.
6. **Length.** Make the Long text match a 30 to 90 second video. Make the Short text match the 15 second cutdown.

## Product name only ("write copy for the Draw Bar Cover")

1. Grep `product-catalogue.md` and fetch the live PDP.
2. List the villain, the 3 to 5 strongest USPs and the audience.
3. Write three texts on three archetypes:
   - Short: problem-frame or personalisation
   - Medium: benefit stack
   - Long: story, cost maths or old vs new

## Creative description or image ("static of a mat on gravel, orange")

1. Describe what the thumb-stopper is in one line: colour, setting, action.
2. The copy must **name what the image shows** in the first line, so the eye and the text agree. Example: gravel in the shot gives "🪨 Gravel can get stuffed."
3. Statics usually want Short or Medium.
4. If an image file is attached, look at it. Pull any on-image text and don't repeat it word for word in the primary text.

## Customer review or testimonial

1. Quote the best line verbatim, in quotation marks. Use no name unless the user confirms permission.
2. Archetype: social-proof lead (12) or customer story (4).
3. Follow with the product's checklist and CTA.
4. Never change the meaning of a quote. Fixing obvious spelling is fine.

## Product page URL

1. Fetch the page. It is the source of truth for specs and price.
2. Translate the PDP language into the ad voice. Strip the corporate words (see `voice-and-rules.md`).
3. Note any difference from the catalogue in the closing flag line.

## Loose idea or brief ("something for farmers about KAJO before harvest")

1. Lock the four basics: product, audience, angle, format. If any are missing, pick sensible defaults and state them in the closing line. Ask only if the product itself is unclear.
2. Use `creative-angles.md` to generate options, then write the strongest three.

## Competitor or inspiration ad

1. Name the mechanic it uses, e.g. a split screen, a POV or a challenge.
2. Rebuild it in DiggerLid's voice with DiggerLid's facts. Never copy their lines.

## Refresh of an existing ad ("this ad is fatiguing, new copy")

1. Keep what's proven: the product, the offer mechanic and the headline style.
2. Change the hook and the archetype. A new first 125 characters is the refresh.
3. Write one variant that keeps the old hook as a control, unless the user says otherwise.
