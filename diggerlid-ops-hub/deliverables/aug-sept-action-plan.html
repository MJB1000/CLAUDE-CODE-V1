The 80/20 — what actually moved profit
Costs improved: VCR fell 7.5pt and CPA fell 27% vs PCP. But MER got worse because AOV fell faster than CPA, and fixed costs rose 51.7% (salaries are 76% of the base and drove 83% of the increase). That alone lifted break-even GPAM from 19.0% to 19.5%. The AOV fall is mix dilution — PRO Mat and couplers — not a cover-unit decline.

The levers, in plain terms

AOV
How big each order is. Raise it by selling more covers/enclosures and bundling a second item. Moves margin %.
CPA
What we pay to get one customer. Lower it by cutting wasteful campaigns. Moves margin %.
CVR
Share of visitors who buy. Raises the dollars, not the margin % — more orders at the same AOV and CPA.
Mix
Which products sell. Covers are ~$250/item; grease tubes ~$14. Mix is the root cause of the AOV fall.
The one equation
GPAM% = (1 − VCR) − CPA ÷ AOV. Only AOV and CPA move the margin percentage. That's why every plan leans on basket size and media efficiency, not conversion rate.

The engine: bundles up, grease spend down

  • Sell slightly more bundles. 69% of orders contain only one product; attaching a second is worth +$129 (+51%). PRO Mat sells alone 91% of the time — the #2 revenue line has no cross-sell offer.
  • Reallocate grease's paid spend. Grease is ~48% of revenue but ~71% of the inferred paid mix. Pull grease prospecting toward ~36% of spend and stop paying premium CPA to acquire low-value grease — grease revenue share drifts ~48% → ~38% and AOV lifts.
  • Stop paying to draw AOV down. Coupler-only orders are 16.3% of orders but 2.4% of revenue — removing them from paid acquisition alone lifts AOV ~16%.

What retention says about all this

From the cohort study (companion report), measured and significance-tested:

  • Grease is a retention engine, not a gateway. It repeats ~2× covers — but grease → >$100 cover is only 5.2% (±2pt), no better than a cover buyer's own repeat. So keep grease as a cheap, high-repeat list engine; don't pay premium CPA betting on migration. This is exactly why we pull grease prospecting spend down.
  • Mats must pay back on order one (2.6% repurchase, p=0.014 vs covers). Fund PRO Mat as volume/list-building, not on an LTV that isn't there.
  • Covers are single-sale-plus-a-small-tail — the bundle attach in Plan C is how we add value to that first (and often only) order.

Recommendation & decision gates

Run Plan C (Attach + Efficiency), with B as the fallback if bundle execution slips. Don't run A — biggest AOV ask, needs a flat line to grow, adds nothing to the BFCM list. C wins on the two things that matter beyond August: +$89.6k over two months and +11% more customers going into Black Friday.

Still on the watch-list

  • Fixed costs up 51.7% vs PCP — salaries drove 83% of it. Bigger than any marketing lever here; belongs on the agenda even though it's out of scope for August.
  • Meta spend by campaign — current grease/cover spend split is inferred from order share, not measured. Deploying the campaign feed makes the reallocation decision-grade (~2 min).
  • Product cost by category — all GPAM maths uses the blended 34.21%; if covers and mats differ materially, the plans shift.
  • Cash flow — August spend $146–163k lands before month-end revenue; BFCM needs ~$225k inside a 10-day window.
