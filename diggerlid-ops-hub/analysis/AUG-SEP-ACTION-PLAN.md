## 2. The four lever combinations

**Targets: August +$20,000 · September +$40,000 · audience must not shrink before BFCM.**
Baseline (July carried forward): 1,676 orders, GPAM 10.4%, **−$34,405**, 1,284 new customers/month.

### ELI5 — what the levers actually are

- **AOV** = how big each order is. Raise it by selling more covers/enclosures, bundling, or getting multiple items per order.
- **CVR** = what share of visitors buy. Raise it by fixing the site/offer.
- **CPA** = what we pay to get one customer. Lower it by cutting wasteful campaigns.
- **Mix** = which products we sell. Covers are $250/item; grease tubes are $14/item.

**Only two of these move margin.** GPAM% = (1 − VCR) − CPA ÷ AOV. **CVR is not in that equation** — more conversions at the same AOV and CPA give the same margin, just more of it. So CVR grows the *dollars*, AOV and CPA grow the *percentage*.

### What each plan asks you to do

**A — MIX-ONLY.** *One thing only: sell a richer product mix.* Push enclosures and covers back to PCP share. No media change, no CVR work.
*Upside:* simplest, single owner. *Risk:* AOV +22% in one month is the biggest single ask of the four, and cover units are currently falling. **Audience flat — no BFCM list growth in August.**

**B — MIX + EFFICIENCY.** *Sell a bit richer, and pay a bit less per customer.* Smaller AOV ask ($290) offset by CPA $87.
*Upside:* **lowest spend ($145,777, $17k below July) and best margin (21.2% GPAM, 32.6% MER).* Best cash profile. *Risk:* **audience stays flat** — you enter BFCM with no more customers than today.

**C — ATTACH + EFFICIENCY.** *Bundle things together, and pay a bit less per customer.* Smallest AOV ask ($283) because bundles lift both AOV and CVR.
*Upside:* **biggest two-month profit (+$89,592) and grows the audience 11%** — the best BFCM position. *Risk:* depends on attach/bundle execution landing, which is untested.

**D — BALANCED.** *A little of everything.* No lever moves more than ~13%.
*Upside:* lowest execution risk per lever; grows audience 8%. *Risk:* four workstreams at once needs coordination.

## 3. Recommendation

**Run C (ATTACH + EFFICIENCY) as the plan, with B as the fallback.**

C wins on the two things that matter beyond August: **$89.6k over two months** and **+11% more new customers going into BFCM**. The AOV ask is also the *smallest* of the four ($283 vs $308 for A) because bundling lifts AOV and CVR together.

**B is the fallback** if bundle execution slips — it delivers $78.6k on $17k less spend, but flat audience.

**Do not run A.** Its AOV ask is the largest, it needs cover units to reverse a 12-month decline, and it adds nothing to the BFCM list.

### Evidence supporting C
- **69% of orders contain only one product.** Attaching a second is worth **+$128.98 (+51%)**.
- **PRO Mat sells alone 91% of the time** — your #2 revenue line has no cross-sell.
- **Digger Wipes, Hydraulic Caps and Bottle Openers never sell alone** — proven attach items, just never offered with mats.
- **Coupler-only orders are 16.3% of orders but 2.4% of revenue** — removing them from *paid acquisition* alone lifts AOV ~16.6%.

## 4. What else belongs in this report before committing

1. **Why cover units are falling.** −33% on Pro Excavator Enclosure is the single biggest driver of the AOV collapse and we have no explanation — stock? price? ad share? competitor? **This is the most important open question and it is unanswered.**
2. **Actual product cost by category.** All GPAM maths uses the blended 34.21%. If mats/covers differ materially, every plan shifts.
3. **Meta spend by campaign.** Current allocation is *inferred* from order share, not measured. The `/api/mer` feed needs a campaign breakdown to make the reallocation real.
4. **Inventory cover for Zip Mat V2 and BFCM.** Plan C drives more units; stocking out mid-campaign wastes committed spend.
5. **Cash-flow profile.** August spend $146k–163k against month-end revenue; BFCM will need ~$225k in a 10-day window.
6. **The G1/G2 cohort question.** Whether coupler-only buyers ever convert is still unanswered (n=4). ~500 more customers would settle it.
7. **Fixed costs.** Up 51.7% vs PCP to $74,831/month — that alone raised break-even GPAM from 19.0% to 19.5%. Worth a separate review; it is the quietest profitability lever.
