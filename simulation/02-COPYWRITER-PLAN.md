## My Approach

**Angle:** Lead with the CFO's daily frustration, not the product. Mid-market CFOs sit through vendor pitches constantly. Another "AI-powered platform" headline will bounce them. I want the headline to describe their problem so precisely they feel seen, then pivot to the solution.

**Headline strategy:** Name the outcome they want. No product name in the headline (per brief flag). No "AI" in the headline either -- save that for the subhead where it explains the mechanism. The headline earns the scroll; the subhead explains what makes it possible.

**Value prop structure:** Three job-to-be-done pain points:
1. Slow monthly close / reporting bottleneck (time waste)
2. Forecast inaccuracy from manual spreadsheet models (trust problem)
3. No visibility between monthly closes (flying blind)

Each heading states the outcome. Each body gives one specific, concrete detail about how Clarifi Analytics delivers it. No fluff.

**Social proof format decision:** Going with two stat-based proof points and one customer quote placeholder. Reasoning:
- CFOs respond to numbers over narrative. Stats are higher-signal for this audience.
- We do not have approved quotes, so a single placeholder quote gives the page structure for when real quotes arrive, without the page depending on them.
- All stats marked [SOURCE NEEDED]. The quote marked [PLACEHOLDER -- REQUIRES REAL CUSTOMER DATA].

**CTA:** The brief locks "Book a Demo" as exact text. Using it verbatim in both placements. No creative deviation here.

**Uncertainties:**
1. No formal brand voice guide was referenced. Writing to the tone description Sofia provided. If there is a brand guide I should see, I need it before I write.
2. Social proof stat placeholders -- I will use realistic-sounding numbers to give the page proper pacing, but every number will be tagged [SOURCE NEEDED]. Confirming this approach is acceptable vs. using abstract placeholders like "[X]%."
