## Audience Patterns

| Audience | Pattern | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredient-conscious women 25-40 (premium skincare) | Curiosity framing ("What if X?") outperforms problem framing ("Your X is broken") in subject lines. This audience is proud of their choices — don't imply they chose wrong. | Dewdrop / 2a — Dana Round 1 |
| Ingredient-conscious women 25-40 (premium skincare) | Education-first sequencing works: awareness > credibility > conversion. Delay the hard CTA until the third touchpoint. | Dewdrop / 2a-2c — Sofia brief |
| Ingredient-conscious women 25-40 (premium skincare) | Ingredient breakdowns formatted as bold-name + one-sentence explanation are scannable on mobile and match the audience's research-driven behavior. | Dewdrop / 2b — Charlie |

## Review Patterns

| Pattern | Source |
|---|---|
| Locked sections in versioned review rounds save re-review time. Round 2 scoped to 6 changed sections vs. 19 total. Scale benefit increases with deliverable complexity. | Dewdrop / 2a-2c — Dana Round 2 |
| [SOURCE NEEDED] blocks must be in a visually separated [DO NOT PUBLISH] section. Inline placeholders are an accidental publish risk. Make this a standard in every brief. | Dewdrop / 2b — Dana Round 1 |
| Anti-disclaimer rule: confident brands do not disclaim their own marketing. "This is not a pressure tactic" / "We hate spam too" / "No hard sell here" all signal the opposite of their intent. | Dewdrop / 2c — Dana Round 1 |

## Channel Patterns

| Channel | Pattern | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Email (DTC product launch) | CTA button text: 4-5 words max. Codes and specifics in body copy, not buttons. | Dewdrop / 2c — Dana Round 1 |
| Email (DTC product launch) | Batch sequences with a shared product differentiator as narrative thread (introduced > explained > referenced) stay cohesive across 3+ emails. | Dewdrop / 2a-2c — Charlie plan |
| Email (DTC product launch) | Sign-off variation across a sequence (Talk soon / [none] / Warmly) creates a warmth gradient without feeling formulaic. | Dewdrop / 2a-2c — Charlie |

## Brief Patterns

| Pattern | Source |
|---|---|
| For skeptical audiences, include subject line tone guidance in the brief. "Never pushy" is insufficient — specify that problem-framing can feel accusatory. | Dewdrop / 2a — Dana Round 1 |
| Always include [SOURCE NEEDED] formatting standards in the brief, not just the tag instruction. Specify: separated section, [DO NOT PUBLISH] header, cannot render accidentally. | Dewdrop / 2b — Dana Round 1 |
| CTA button text length should be a standard constraint in email briefs (4-5 words). | Dewdrop / 2c — Dana Round 1 |
| Batch briefing with labeled sub-deliverables (2a, 2b, 2c) and a shared narrative arc works for related emails. Include the arc in the brief, not just individual email specs. | Dewdrop / 2a-2c — Sofia brief |

## Anti-Patterns (Things That Failed)

| What Happened | Why It Failed | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Subject line "The reason your Vitamin C serum stopped working" | Framed as reader's personal failure — alienates an audience proud of their skincare knowledge | For self-educated audiences, frame problems as industry limitations, not personal mistakes |
| "This is not a pressure tactic" in urgency email | Defensive disclaimers signal insecurity. The Streisand effect applies to marketing copy. | Trust the tone. If the copy reads warm, adding a disclaimer makes it read cold. |
| Inline [SOURCE NEEDED] tags in email body | Looks like real copy. If accidentally published, readers see placeholder text. | Always separate unverified content with a publishing gate. |
