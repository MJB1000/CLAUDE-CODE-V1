## What Copywriter Got Right First Time

- Airless pump as narrative thread across all three emails — cohesive and differentiated
- Progressive arc (Problem > Proof > Action) matched audience journey naturally
- GLOW20 distribution: prominent in Emails 1 and 3, soft P.S. in Email 2 — per Sofia's direction
- [SOURCE NEEDED] tags correctly placed for all unverified claims (placement was wrong, content flagging was right)
- "Your skin" framing maintained consistently across all three emails
- Ingredient breakdown format in Email 2 (bold name + one sentence) — scannable on mobile
- Sign-off variation (Talk soon / none / Warmly) created warmth gradient

## What the Brief Should Have Included

- Subject line tone guidance: brief said "never pushy" but did not specify that problem-framing subject lines can feel accusatory for this audience. Future briefs for skeptical audiences should flag this.
- [SOURCE NEEDED] formatting standards: brief said to use the tag but did not specify HOW to format them to prevent accidental publish. Should be a standard operating procedure.
- CTA text length guideline: brief specified "CTA button text" but did not mention mobile button text length best practices.

## Rules to Carry Forward

1. For skeptical, ingredient-conscious audiences: curiosity framing ("What if X?") outperforms problem framing ("Your X is broken") in subject lines.
2. All [SOURCE NEEDED] content must be in a separated [DO NOT PUBLISH] section — never inline with body copy.
3. Never disclaim your own offer. If the tone is warm, the copy will read as warm. Adding "this is not a pressure tactic" signals the opposite.
4. CTA button text should be 4-5 words max. Codes and details belong in body copy, not buttons.
5. Batch deliverables with a shared narrative thread stay cohesive. The airless pump thread worked because it was introduced, explained, and referenced — not just repeated.
6. Locked sections in review rounds save significant re-review time. Round 2 scoped to 6 changed sections instead of all 19.
