## Round 1 — 2026-04-02

Ready for Copywriter: NO

### Must Fix

1. **03-EMAIL-1-LAUNCH.md:subject line** — "The reason your Vitamin C serum stopped working" implies the reader's current product is defective. For an audience that is ingredient-conscious and already loyal to their routines, this risks feeling like an attack on their judgment rather than an invitation. — Rewrite to frame the problem as an industry-wide limitation, not a personal failure. Example direction: focus on what they are missing out on, not what they got wrong.

2. **04-EMAIL-2-SOCIAL-PROOF.md:clinical data section** — The two [SOURCE NEEDED] blocks are currently inline with body copy, making them read as part of the email. If this were accidentally published without replacement, the reader would see placeholder text. — Move [SOURCE NEEDED] blocks to a clearly separated section below the body copy, or wrap them in a comment-style format that cannot accidentally render. Add a note: "Do not publish until these are replaced."

3. **05-EMAIL-3-URGENCY.md:body** — "This is not a pressure tactic" is defensive. The brand voice is warm and confident — a confident brand does not need to disclaim its own offer. The sentence draws attention to the exact thing it tries to deny. — Remove the sentence. The surrounding copy already achieves the non-pushy tone without it. The warmth of "If now is not the right time, the serum will be here when you are ready" is sufficient.

### Should Fix

1. **04-EMAIL-2-SOCIAL-PROOF.md:Ferulic Acid description** — "Your sunscreen's best friend" is a cliche that undercuts the otherwise intelligent, science-forward tone. — Rephrase to something specific: e.g., "Doubles down on UV defense when paired with your daily SPF."

2. **03-EMAIL-1-LAUNCH.md:body** — "That amber color in your dropper bottle? That is oxidation." — Strong line, but "amber" may confuse readers whose serums are intentionally amber-toned (some formulations are). — Consider "that darker color" or "that color change" to avoid ambiguity.

3. **05-EMAIL-3-URGENCY.md:CTA** — "Get 20% Off with GLOW20" includes the code in the button text. Best practice for email CTAs is to keep button text short and action-oriented. Code should be visible in the body copy (which it is). — Consider shortening to "Shop the Launch Offer" or "Claim Your 20% Off."
